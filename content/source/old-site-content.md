The Friends of Grace Dieu Priory would like to thank everyone who attended our 20th Anniversary Medieval Fayre which was a huge success.




After a very hot spell, the 28th of June 2026 was forecast to be a pleasant 250C just right for the Friends of Grace Dieu Priory Medieval Fayre. We have looked after the site for 20 years and wanted to celebrate this with an event the community could share with us, which is why we chose to keep the event free. After months of preparation the final two days on the site had temperatures of over 300C, but spraying out pitches for traders and car parking were ready!

At 7am we opened the gates and soon the first re-enactment groups and traders began to arrive and the site was soon starting to buzz with activity. Before our eyes the priory was transformed into a medieval camp taking us back to 1485, tents sprang up hosting an armoury alongside period food and crafts whilst in the field opposite arrows began to fly as the archers began to practice and drill. History was brought to life as costumed villagers and soldiers roamed the ruins. At the same time, a market was set up around the site as local traders put up their stalls with a wide variety of wares including honey from Mount St Bernard's Abbey, jewellery, cakes and bakes, crafts and a treasure trove of Viking goods.

The fayre opened to the public at 10:30 and within a short time the priory was a bustle of activity, visitors were encouraged to try on armour and join in with squire training, firing arrows and having a go at wood turning using period tools. The Sing It Happy Choir delighted the audience in the cloisters. Behind the Tudor kitchen the coffee van, pizza and burgers provided refreshments and the ice cream van provided welcome treats for all. There was plenty of laughter and cheering as the enactors performed their traditional Mummers play, St George slew the Dragon and the fair maid was saved! The archers provided a fabulous display and the final battle was waged with daring, skill and humour.

The Friends of Grace Dieu Priory are a small group of volunteers who manage the ruins and site to ensure it remains open to the public. Our volunteer car park marshals did a sterling job guiding visitors to spaces in Rusty Mary’s field throughout the day and we would like to thank everyone who volunteered and helped us run the day, to the fabulous re-enactment groups, the Friends of the Thomas Grey Household and the Ferrers Household along with Charnwood Geopark and their volunteers, Leicester University and the Remet Company, Shepshed along with all the volunteer parking crew! A huge thank you to the community for coming along and supporting the day and leaving the site so clean and tidy.

From Castles to Cloisters: the Remarkable Life of Roesia de Verdun

Below is an article that appeared in the October 2025 edition of the Belton Maypole magazine

 

In 1995 Grace Dieu Priory was a pile of stone covered in ivy. A number of people got together to save what was left, and the Grace Dieu Priory Charitable Trust company was born. The Trust now own the Priory and Rusty Mary field.

During 1996 to 2006 conservation work was carried out and after many setbacks opened the Priory to the public. The Friends of Grace Dieu Priory was formed and registered as a Charity to carry out day-to-day tasks. In the last 20 years the Priory has hosted thousands of visitors at events organised by volunteers with no direct funding from any other body.

The initial group is now aging and struggling to maintain the necessary level of activity. The Priory is financially sound and we hope to build a new bridge from the Rusty Mary field into the Priory. Our next annual Carol Concert is planned for the 7th of December and next year we will be doing our archaeology events and walks again.

This article is an appeal to anyone who has an interest in local history or who would just like to give a few hours per year to keep the Priory going. We are not going to lock the gates in the near future but unless we can recruit some help it could happen.

So, if you have any office skills or a bit of muscle to help put up the gazebos at events, please become a friend. We do have younger members also but would like to have more. You would be made very welcome if you would come and join The Friends of Grace Dieu Priory.

Please note that the price of Dame Petronella's 1414-1418 Account book has been reduced to £15 + p&p.

The book is unusual in that it is the sort of record which lay behind formal accounts and which tended to get destroyed.
It is clear someone has later annotated many of the entries with the heading of the classic manorial account.
The book details both the income of the priory - mostly rents from settlements within a few miles of the house - and its expenditure.
The latter included purchases of meat and fish and other foodstuffs for the household, costs of items such as building work, agriculture (mostly wages) and payments to servants. The Account Book is not just about money, but also a source of information about many aspects of social life at the beginning of the 15th century. The Draft Account Book will be invaluable to medievalists studying social, economic and monastic history and to Family Historians.
 
The book has been printed in paperback form, in a limited edition of 300. Professor Christopher Dyer of the University of Leicester has written the Preface; Kenneth Hillier, a trustee and local Historian, has written a 29 page Introduction; Dominic Johnson, Genealogist and Historical Researcher is responsible for the transcription and translation from the original medieval Latin; and the late Lesley Boatwright wrote on the Editorial Conventions and Latinity of the Grace Dieu Priory clerks. There is a Glossary of both Latin and medieval English words; Person, Places and Subjects indexes; and a useful list of Saints Days and Festivals.
