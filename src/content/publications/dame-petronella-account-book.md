This rare historical volume preserves the working drafts that lay behind the formal monastic accounts—documents that typically perished over time. Later annotated with headings of classic manorial accounts, the text details both priory income (principally rents from nearby settlements) and daily expenditures.

Purchases recorded include meat, fish, household foodstuffs, masonry building works, agricultural wages, and servants' stipends. It provides an extraordinary window into early 15th-century English social, economic, and monastic life.

### Scholarly Apparatus
- **Preface:** Professor Christopher Dyer (University of Leicester).
- **Introduction:** 29-page historical study by trustee Kenneth Hillier.
- **Transcription & Translation:** Rendered from medieval Latin by genealogist Dominic Johnson.
- **Editorial Conventions:** Notes on the Latinity of the priory clerks by the late Lesley Boatwright.
- Complete glossary of medieval English and Latin terminology, comprehensive indices of persons, places, and subjects, and calendar of Saints' Days and holy festivals.
